The formula, with your inputs substituted
K  =  [ LGD · N( G(PD) / √(1−R)  +  √(R/(1−R)) · G(0.999) )  −  PD · LGD ]  ·  MA

Reading it. The inner N(·) is a conditional default probability: the share of the portfolio that defaults when the single systematic factor lands at its 99.9th-percentile worst outcome. Subtracting PD · LGD strips out expected loss, which is covered by provisions rather than capital — so K is unexpected loss alone.

Why the curve bends. Correlation R falls from 0.24 to 0.12 as PD rises: a borrower already close to default is driven more by its own circumstances than by the economy. That decay is what flattens the right-hand side of the curve.

Retail classes carry no maturity adjustment. The 1.06 scaling factor from Basel II is not applied. The dotted rule marks the 0.03% PD floor under CRR Art. 160(1) and 163(1). Illustrative only — not a substitute for the regulation.
